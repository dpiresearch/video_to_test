# TC-RF-001: Over-the-air detection of a 436.5 MHz UHF emission with tinySA and DUT indicator response

**Objective:** Confirm that when the RF link is active, the tinySA detects a carrier near 436.5 MHz clearly above the background, and that the LTM-1C board's status LED turns on.

## Summary of the recorded run

A handheld camera records a bench setup: a green PCB stack labelled "LTM-1C" with a telescopic antenna on an SMA connector, a tinySA spectrum analyzer with a stubby antenna sweeping 240–959 MHz, and a handheld UHF transceiver (lower left). At first the tinySA's highest peak is a background signal near 502 MHz at about −51 dBm. At about 2.5–2.8 s the transceiver's display lights up blue and a green LED comes on on the board. By about 3.8 s the analyzer's peak marker has jumped to about 436.5 MHz at −46.9 to −50.4 dBm and stays there. The transceiver backlight goes off near the end (~7.4–7.9 s). The green LED stays on.

## Equipment

- **tinySA spectrum analyzer (HIGH input mode, sweep START 240 MHz / STOP ~959 MHz, RBW ~621 kHz, marker 1 on peak)**: Measures the over-the-air RF spectrum and peak level
- **Stubby rubber-duck antenna with gold SMA on tinySA**: Receive antenna for the analyzer
- **LTM-1C board stack with telescopic antenna on SMA**: Device under test
- **Handheld UHF transceiver (VFO/MR keypad radio)**: RF source / companion radio
- **USB cables (T4, T8)**: Power the instrument and the DUT

## Test procedure

### Preconditions

- The tinySA is charged or USB-powered and has been calibrated.
- The LTM-1C board is powered over USB and its firmware is loaded.
- The handheld UHF transceiver is set to about 436.5 MHz (70 cm band), and the operator holds a valid licence or uses a dummy load/low power as required.
- The bench area has no other strong transmitters in the 430–440 MHz range.

### Setup

- Fit the stubby antenna to the tinySA RF input and set the sweep to START 240 MHz / STOP ~959 MHz, HIGH mode, RBW auto (~621 kHz), marker 1 = peak search.
- Fit the telescopic antenna to the LTM-1C SMA connector and lay it horizontally toward the tinySA.
- Place the handheld transceiver within about 30 cm of the tinySA antenna and the board antenna.
- Check that the board's green status LED is off before starting.

### Pass criteria

- The tinySA marker reads 436.5 MHz ±0.5 MHz while the link is active.
- The peak level is about −50 dBm or higher at the given distance and stands out from the background.
- The board's green LED lights when the RF activity starts.
- The peak is absent, or back at the background level, before the activity starts.

### Fail criteria

- No peak near 436.5 MHz while the transceiver/DUT is active.
- The peak is off-frequency by more than 0.5 MHz.
- The board LED does not light during the activity.
- The signal is unstable or keeps dropping out during the hold period.

### Teardown

- Unkey the transceiver and switch it off.
- Save or screenshot the tinySA trace if needed, then return the analyzer to its default sweep.
- Power down the LTM-1C board and disconnect the USB cables.
- Remove the antennas and store them.

## Assumptions

- The roles are inferred: the handheld transceiver is assumed to be the RF source (its backlight turns on at the same time as the 436.5 MHz peak appears). The LTM-1C board might instead be the transmitter, with the radio as receiver or monitor. The video cannot tell which.
- No hands were detected, so the triggering action (PTT press, software command) happened off-screen.
- The green LED's meaning (signal detected, transmitting, or plain activity) is not labelled. Whether it stays latched after the activity cannot be confirmed in the 8 s clip.
- The tinySA readings (502 MHz/−52.4 dBm, 436.5 MHz/−48.4 dBm, etc.) are read from small on-screen text and may be off by ±0.1 MHz or ±0.1 dB.
- The pass threshold levels and the ±0.5 MHz tolerance come from the observed run, not from a documented specification.
- The appearance of T10/T11 and the T7 size changes come from handheld camera motion, not from changes in the setup.
